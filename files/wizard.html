Where do I get games?


The kit doesn't come with any games - you bring your own, and only ones you're allowed to use. The honest ways to get them:
    

  • Your own boards and cartridges. If you own the machine or cartridge, its chips can be copied ("dumped") for your own use.
    • 

  • Games given away free by their owners. The MAME team keeps a page of arcade games whose owners have said they can be shared free (mamedev.org/roms). Atari 2600 fans are still making brand new games ("homebrew") - many are free to download from their makers.


Arcade games go in as the .zip file just as it is - don't unzip it. Atari 2600 games are .bin or .a26 files. The kit works out which game it is from what's inside, so the file name doesn't matter, and it shows the game's proper name.


What the lists mean


Score: automatic the kit already knows where the game keeps its score, so you can train straight away.

Score: you need to find it before an AI can learn it, play the game for a few minutes in the Find the score window and type the score in now and then. You only do this once per game.

Won't run that copy of the game was made for a different emulator, so it won't load here.


Watching and teaching
    

  • Watch it play opens a window showing the AI's best brain playing live. M turns the sound on and off.
    • 

  • Press L in that window to take over and show it how: arrows to move, Z X C A S D for buttons. Press L again to hand back. Next time it trains, it copies you first.
    • 

  • F1 shows the help in any game window.


Find the score keys
    

  • 5 coin, 1 start, arrows and Z-D to play.
    • 

  • P then type the score you can see. Do it two or three times at different scores.
    • 

  • G at GAME OVER, then F to search. Y saves what it found, N tries the next one.
    • 

  • Fighting games: H round starts, J you got hit, K you hit them, U nothing happened.

About Arcade Apprentice


Hiya, I'm Jonny. I run a little shop on Dickson Road in Blackpool, and when it's quiet I tinker with AI.


I wanted to watch a computer learn to play a game from nothing. It doesn't know what the buttons do, it doesn't know what a ghost is, it just has a go thousands of times until it gets good. It's daft how addictive it is to watch. So I built this kit, and then I thought, why keep it to myself?


It's free. No adverts, no sign-ups, and nothing leaves your computer. Bring your own games, pick one, press go, and turn the dials to see what happens.

Settings


Changes are used the next time you press Train (if it's training now: Stop, then Train). Tip: save a checkpoint first, so you can go back if a tweak makes it worse.




Recipe cards


Share the settings and results, never the ROM. Export shows exactly what goes in the file first. Import lets you tick the dials to keep and backs up first.








Checkpoints 


A checkpoint is a saved copy of the brain and its settings. Restore one to go back to that moment.

Teach another AI 


This AI plays a few hundred games while another AI on the same game takes notes - next time that one trains, it copies this one first.




Check & repair 


If the PC crashed or lost power while it was training, this checks its files and fixes what it can. Copies of everything are kept first.




Start again


Wipes what it has learned and starts from scratch with the current settings. The old run is kept in the AI's files\old runs folder, just in case.
